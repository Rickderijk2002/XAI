## **General**

### **Points Distribution** 

| Component | Max Points |
| :---- | :---- |
| Design Report | 40 |
| Design Archive | 20 |
| Design Market (presentation) | 30 |
| **Total** | **90** |

#### **Design Market (30 pts)**

This is **one third of our grade** and it's mostly about telling a good story and having a visually compelling dashboard to show. If our dashboard looks good and we present confidently, this is almost free points. Don't underestimate it.

#### **Archive (20 pts)**

This is almost entirely about **documentation discipline**, not creative work. 12 of those points are just: did you save your raw materials, interview notes, screenshots, decisions? If we document as we go, this costs almost nothing.

#### **Report (40 pts)** 

* **Ideate, Prototype, Test**: 8 pts each,  these three are where most points are  
* **Empathize**: 6 pts  
* **Process \+ Design concept**: 4 pts each, don't over-invest here  
* **Define**: 2 pts, almost a freebie

The dashboard is the core artifact. If we build a genuinely good dashboard, it covers Prototype (8pts), feeds Test (8pts), and carries the Design Market (30pts). That's potentially **46 of 90 points** essentially coming from one deliverable.

## **Points Overview**

| :---- | :---- | :---- |

| **Define** | **2 pts** | The insights gained from user research were used to define the problem space, which were taken as a clear and well explained focus for the ideation phase.  |

## **The Report Outline**

### **1 Introduction (1-2 pages)**

*This section sets the scene. Keep it concise.*  
 

#### 1.1 Assignment Description

Write: What is the assignment? Briefly describe that this is Assignment B — building an interactive dashboard to explore counterfactual explanation data. Mention the context: XAI, MNIST classifier, counterfactual methods.  
 

#### 1.2 Design Debrief

Write: A short summary of what you set out to design and for whom. Introduce the core tension: validity vs. plausibility. State your target user (Data Science student / ML researcher) and the core problem you are solving.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Process — 2 pts):**  
The report must be clearly structured according to all five design thinking phases. This introduction should make clear that you followed that process.

### **2 Empathize Phase (2-4 pages)**

*Goal: Show that you genuinely understood the problem space and user before designing anything.*  
 

#### 2.1 Methods Used

Write: Describe which methods you used to understand the problem. 

•   	Affinity mapping / brainstorm session with post-its  
•   	Literature scan of existing XAI dashboard tools  
•   	Short user interview (optional but adds points)  
   
For each method: explain what you did, who was involved, when, and why you chose it.  
 

#### 2.2 Results & Insights

Write: What did you learn? Summarize 3–5 key insights about your user and the problem space. These insights must feed directly into your Define phase. Example insights:  
•   	Users struggle to distinguish visually plausible vs. valid counterfactuals without side-by-side comparison  
•   	Researchers care more about method comparison than individual image inspection  
•   	Timeout cases are a common point of confusion in existing tools  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Empathize — 6 pts):**  
Good score requires: 2–3 well-motivated methods AND highly relevant insights. Just listing what you did is not enough — explain why and what you concluded.  
   
**📁 Archive: Save these**  
•   	Photos of affinity map / post-it wall  
•   	Notes or bullet points from literature scan (3–5 sources minimum)  
•   	Interview notes if conducted

### **3 Define Phase (1 page)**

*Goal: Turn your empathy insights into a focused problem statement.*

#### 3.1 Problem Statement

Write: A crisp 1–2 sentence problem statement. Format suggestion:  
"\[Target user\] needs a way to \[do X\] because \[insight from empathy phase\], but currently \[gap/problem\]."

#### 3.2 Target User

Write: Who are you designing for? Describe your primary user (Data Science student) and secondary user (ML researcher). What do they need from the dashboard? What do they find confusing or difficult?

#### 3.3 Focus for Ideation

Write: Explicitly state which XAI challenge and user need you chose to focus on going into ideation, and why. This is the bridge between empathy and ideas.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Define — 2 pts):**  
The insights from Empathize must visibly drive this section. Don't define a problem that appeared from nowhere — trace it back to your research.  
   
**📁 Archive: Save these**  
•   	Notes from your define session  
•   	The final problem statement (can just be a screenshot or note)  
 

### **4 Ideation Phase (3-4 pages)**

*Goal: Show that you explored widely before converging. Diversity matters here.*

#### 4.1 Divergence — Generating Ideas

Write: Describe your brainwriting process (everyone came with ideas independently, then shared). List all ideas generated — aim for at least 6–8 distinct concepts. Include rough sketches or descriptions. Ideas should genuinely differ from each other.  
   
Briefly describe each idea — what is the core view, who is it for, what question does it answer?

#### 4.2 COCD Box

Write: Explain the COCD box technique and include a photo/image of your COCD box. Describe how ideas were placed and why.  
   
The COCD box has two axes: feasibility and originality. Describe which quadrant each idea landed in.

#### 4.3 Convergence — Chosen Direction

Write: Which idea (or combination) did you choose and why? The argument must be grounded in your user needs and the XAI problem, not just team preference. Reference your Define insights explicitly.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Ideate — 8 pts):**  
Good score requires: wide diversity of ideas \+ explicit user/XAI-based reasoning for your choice. A COCD box with only similar ideas, or a choice made without clear rationale, will score Acceptable at best.  
   
**📁 Archive: Save these**  
•   	Photo of COCD box  
•   	All sketches or written descriptions of ideas  
•   	Notes from your convergence decision

### 

### **5 Prototype Phase (2-3 pages)**

*Goal: Document the dashboard you built and the design decisions behind it.*

#### 5.1 Prototype Description

Write: Describe what you built. What does the dashboard show? What interactions does it support? How is it structured (views, filters, panels)?

#### 5.2 Design Decisions

Write: Explain key design choices and why you made them. Examples:  
•   	Why did you choose this primary view?  
•   	How did you decide to visualize the validity/plausibility tension?  
•   	Why these filters and not others?  
   
Connect each decision back to your user needs from Define.

#### 5.3 Prototype Screenshots

Include: Screenshots of all key views and interactions. Label them clearly.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Prototype — 8 pts):**  
Good score requires: the dashboard uses real data, allows real interaction, and has appropriate fidelity for testing. A static mockup will not score well here.  
   
**📁 Archive: Save these**  
•   	Any wireframes or sketches made before building  
•   	Screenshots of intermediate versions  
•   	Link to the working dashboard or the dashboard file itself

### **6 Test Phase (4 pages)**

*Goal: Show you tested with real users and drew real insights from it.*

#### 6.1 Research Question

Write: What did you want to learn from testing? State this clearly. Example: "Can users correctly identify the validity/plausibility tension in the dashboard without guidance?"

#### 6.2 Method

Write: How did you test? Describe:  
•   	Who your participants were (3–5 is sufficient, describe their profile)  
•   	What tasks you gave them (include the exact task formulations)  
•   	What questions you asked after each task or at the end  
•   	How you recorded observations (notes, screen recording, etc.)  
 6.3 Results  
Write: What did you observe? Summarize findings per participant or per task. Be specific — not just 'users were confused' but what exactly caused confusion and when.

#### 6.4 Insights

📝 Write: What are the 3–5 key takeaways from testing? These feed directly into your recommendations.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Test — 8 pts):**  
Good score requires: structured test with explicit goals, multiple users, documented insights, and a write-up that allows someone else to repeat the study. A casual 'we showed it to a friend' will not score well.  
   
**📁 Archive: Save these**  
•   	Test protocol document (tasks \+ questions written out)  
•   	Raw notes per participant — unprocessed, as taken during sessions  
•   	Screen recordings if captured (bonus, not required)

### **7 Conclusions & Recommendations (4 pages)**

*Goal: Show what you learned and what the improved design would look like.*

#### 7.1 Conclusions from User Testing

Write: What did testing confirm or challenge about your design? What worked well, what did not? Be honest — the rubric rewards reflection, not perfection.

#### 7.2 Improvement Recommendations

Write: Based on your test insights, what would you change in a second iteration? Be specific. You do NOT need to rebuild — just describe and visualize the improvements.

#### 7.3 Final Design Concept

Write: Present the final dashboard with annotations or updated screenshots. Describe what it does and how it meets your user needs from Define.

#### 7.4 User Interaction in Context

Write \+ Visualize: Show how a real user would interact with the dashboard in their actual use context. Example: 'A Data Science student is comparing PIECE vs. Alibi-CF on digit 7 to understand why PIECE has high implausibility...' Include a screenshot or sketch showing the user journey.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Design Concept — 4 pts):**  
Good score requires: clear visuals of the final design AND a scenario showing actual user interaction in context. The connection to user needs must be explicit.

### **8 Design Archive Description (0.5 page)**

*This section in the report just describes your archive — the actual archive lives in your folder.*

#### 8.1 Archive Overview

Write: A short description of what the archive contains and where to find what. Reference your folder structure explicitly.  
   
**⚠️ Rubric check (Archive — 20 pts):**  
Structure (8 pts): Is the archive clearly organized and navigable? Use a logical folder structure with clear names.  
Transparency (12 pts): Are all materials, methods and raw data present? Raw \= unprocessed. Notes taken during testing count. Cleaned-up summaries do not replace raw notes.

•   	README.txt is mandatory — it is the first thing graders will look at
